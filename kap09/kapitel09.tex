\section{Discussion and Practical Guidance}
\label{ch:discussion}

\subsection{Method selection}
KDE is appropriate when uncertainty is available through data, is non-Gaussian or
multimodal, and the optimizer requires a smooth scalar constraint. The evidence is
strongest for low-dimensional residuals. A Gaussian reformulation is preferable
when its distributional and convexity assumptions hold; scenario methods remain
preferable when an a priori finite-sample feasibility statement is required.

\subsection{Bandwidth and sample size}
Silverman's rule is a starting value, not an optimization guarantee. In the local
fixed-residual sweep, multiplying the baseline bandwidth by $0.25$ to $4$ changed
the KDE violation estimate from $0.3765$ to $0.4234$, while the independent test
frequency was $0.3950$. The sample-size sweep ($N=50$ through $2000$) produced
estimates between $0.3754$ and $0.4284$. These are estimator diagnostics at a fixed
residual distribution, not reoptimized decisions. They
support a stability check around the baseline rather than a claim that increasing
$N$ alone forces a monotone approach to $\varepsilon$. A logarithmic sweep should
be followed by continuation from larger to smaller bandwidths when the NLP is
sensitive. Rare-event levels and high residual dimensions require methods designed
for tail estimation or distributional robustness.

\subsection{Limits and open problems}
Schuster's convergence results apply under their stated assumptions and to the
estimator covered by the theorem. They do not supply a universal finite-sample
out-of-sample certificate, and the convergence gap for the biased construction
remains open. Distribution shift, optimization-aware bandwidth selection,
finite-sample biased-KDE bounds, and sample compression for real-time control are
natural next problems. The dispatch benchmark supports portability of the pipeline.
The lunar proxy uses a different horizon and Euler mesh from the LGR
implementation of \citet{keil2020,keil2021}; its cost near $30$ and failed robust stages do not reproduce the
published costs near $9.09$. This difference is reported rather than interpreted
as a contradiction of the published experiment.
