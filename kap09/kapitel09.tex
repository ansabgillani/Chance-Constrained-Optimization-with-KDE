\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{ch:experiments}

This chapter reports the local evidence rather than treating every output field as a theorem. The analytical formulation question (RQ1) is established in Chapters~4--6. The experiments address RQ2 by comparing nominal, scenario, unbiased KDE, and biased KDE (shifted Epanechnikov kernel, $b(h)=h$) constraints across residual laws and a balance-constrained dispatch adapter; RQ3 by varying bandwidth and nested sample size; and RQ4 by comparing scalar joint-event statistics. The lunar continuation is a separate audit objective that establishes what the local transcription can support after its numerical checks. All experiments are synthetic, seeded, and run locally. The resulting measurements are empirical frequencies or estimator diagnostics; they are not out-of-sample certificates.

\subsection{Design, Estimands, and Provenance}

The static model has two decision variables and a nonlinear residual whose uncertainty enters through a scalar residual map. Four residual laws are used: Gaussian, bimodal, skewed, and heavy-tailed. For each law, 250 training observations define the optimization constraint and 4000 independent test observations define the held-out frequency of Eq.~\eqref{eq:test-violation}. The target is $\varepsilon=0.1$. The method order is nominal, scenario, unbiased KDE, and biased KDE. The KDE rows use the bandwidth of Silverman's robust rule, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch3-bandwidth-rule}, computed once before optimization from the first uncertainty coordinate of the 250 training observations in the static rows and from the 250 renewable-error training samples in the dispatch rows; the stored values are $h\approx0.295$, $0.376$, $0.242$, and $0.277$ for the Gaussian, bimodal, skewed, and heavy-tailed laws and $h\approx0.093$ for dispatch. The exact seed mapping is $20260906$ for the Gaussian static run, $20260907$ for dispatch and the bimodal row, $20260908$ for the joint comparison and the skewed row, $20260909$ for sensitivity and the heavy-tailed row, and $20260910$ for the lunar continuation. The mapping is recorded explicitly because a statement such as “the five benchmark seeds” is otherwise not reproducible.

The result ledger contains objective, training violation, held-out violation, estimated violation, solver status, and gap fields. The original runner had a reporting defect: it stored a Gaussian diagnostic under \texttt{estimated\_violation} for the biased KDE. The corrected runner now reports the same biased KDE that appears in the NLP; its successful rows are active at approximately $0.1$. The older records remain preserved as an audit artifact, while the corrected and paper-aligned records are used for the comparisons below. This correction changes the interpretation of the estimator field but does not change the stored decisions or held-out frequencies.

Each experiment changes one declared part of the method and has one primary
estimand and one validation gate. The static comparison changes the estimator
and residual law and measures objective together with held-out violation; solver
status and an independent Gaussian check are required. The paper-aligned
sequence changes $N$ and $h_N$ and follows the decision path over nested
prefixes against a fixed independent test set. The dispatch transfer changes the
model adapter and estimator and measures objective and balance violation against
the exact mixture CDF. The joint experiment changes only the scalar event map and
measures the test union frequency after the component and event definitions are
checked. The lunar continuation changes the continuation stage and records
feasibility and the audit invariant, with solver status (including the
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions) and path scope as gates. A result is not
promoted to a method claim when its gate fails.

The qualitative pattern is compatible with the motivation for KDE in process systems and data-driven chance constraints \citep{calfa2015}: a residual-space estimator can represent multimodality without committing to a single Gaussian family. It does not follow that KDE dominates scenario optimization in every regime. Scenario optimization has a different guarantee architecture, with explicit sample-complexity conditions for convex programs \citep{calafiore2006,campi2008}. The local nonlinear problem is outside the setting in which those results can simply be quoted.

\subsection{Paper-Aligned Hyperparameter Experiment}

The first question is whether the qualitative conclusions survive a bandwidth and sample-size regime that is recognizably related to the literature. The convergence work of \citet{schuster2025} does not prescribe a finite numerical bandwidth; it requires a shrinking bandwidth with increasing sample size and gives a Gaussian product kernel with diagonal bandwidth as a concrete route. The related numerical work uses the normal-reference scaling $h_N=1.06\hat\sigma_NN^{-1/5}$ in one dimension and large independent Monte Carlo samples \citep{schuster2022}, where $\hat\sigma_N$ is the sample standard deviation of the $N$ training samples to which the bandwidth is applied. \citet{keil2020,keil2021,keil2021warm} specify a different object: the one-sided Epanechnikov bias $b(h)=h$, risk levels $\varepsilon_a=0.1$ and $\varepsilon_b=0.01$, and continuation in bandwidth and sample size inside an LGR optimal-control calculation.

The local profile implements only the portable parts, as a paper-aligned unbiased KDE and a paper-aligned biased KDE. It uses nested IID prefixes of $N\in\{250,1000,5000\}$, a fixed independent test set of 10,000 observations, the scalar Schuster bandwidth scaling, with $d=1$ the dimension of the estimated variable (the scalar residual) and $\hat\sigma_N$ computed from the first uncertainty coordinate of the training prefix in the static rows and from the renewable-error prefix in the dispatch rows,
\begin{equation}
 h_N=\hat\sigma_N\left(\frac{4}{(d+2)N}\right)^{1/(d+4)},
 \qquad d=1,
 \label{eq:schuster-local-bandwidth}
\end{equation}
and the one-sided Epanechnikov translation $b(h)=h$ of Keil et al. The paper-aligned biased KDE tries bandwidth multipliers $(0.25,0.5,1,2)$ and warm-starts each attempt. It does not use Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC), the 50,000-sample Keil chain, LGR nodes, SNOPT, or mesh-error triggers. The output is consequently a scale-normalized local comparison, not a reproduction of either paper. The paper-aligned biased KDE rows (Table~\ref{tab:paper-aligned-sequence}, Figures~\ref{fig:paper-aligned-static} and~\ref{fig:paper-aligned-dispatch}) and the biased KDE rows of the main runs (Tables~\ref{tab:local-static-results}, \ref{tab:dispatch-results}, and~\ref{tab:lunar-results}) use the same shifted kernel with $b(h)=h$ and differ in the bandwidth rule and continuation, which are Schuster scaling with the multiplier continuation in this profile and a single Silverman bandwidth without bandwidth continuation in the main rows.

The question is whether increasing $N$ makes the two estimators approach the same fitted decision. In the Gaussian static row, the paper-aligned unbiased KDE objective changes from $0.2270$ at $N=250$ to $0.2016$ and $0.1599$ at $N=1000$ and $N=5000$, with test violations $0.0464$, $0.0586$, and $0.0834$. The paper-aligned biased KDE objective is $0.3623$, $0.3077$, and $0.4100$, with test violations $0.0138$, $0.0228$, and $0.0091$. The observation is not monotone convergence: the Gaussian decisions move toward the empirical target as smoothing decreases, whereas the biased decisions remain more conservative and change bandwidth branch at $N=5000$. The mechanism is compatible with the one-sided support shift: $b(h)=h$ eliminates optimistic contributions from positive residuals, while the Gaussian estimator allows smooth mass on both sides of the threshold. Because the biased arm also changes the selected bandwidth and continuation branch, this comparison does not identify the causal effect of kernel bias alone.

The distribution-shape comparison gives the same analytical distinction. At $N=5000$, paper-aligned unbiased KDE test violations are $0.0548$ for bimodal, $0.0910$ for skewed, and $0.0794$ for heavy-tailed residuals. The corresponding paper-aligned biased KDE values are $0$, $0.0383$, and $0.0275$, with objectives $0.8223$, $0.4677$, and $0.7202$. Figure~\ref{fig:paper-aligned-static} shows the held-out violations of both sequences for all four laws. The paper-aligned biased KDE is safer on this test split but more conservative. This is consistent with the pointwise upper-bound construction of Keil et al., but it does not validate Schuster's optimizer convergence theorem: no uniform residual-grid error was computed, the feasible decision was not shown to converge, and the static objective does not satisfy all of Schuster's assumptions.

\subsection{Energy Dispatch and Exact Distributional Check}

The dispatch proxy has two generators and a balance residual. Capacity residuals are deterministic in this adapter, so the stochastic problem tests only renewable forecast error in the balance equation. The error is the mixture $0.6\mathcal N(-0.25,0.10^2)+0.4\mathcal N(0.35,0.15^2)$. Table~\ref{tab:dispatch-results} reports the objective values and held-out frequencies.

Because the mixture is known, its CDF supplies an independent reference. The exact mixture probability at the nominal decision is approximately $0.5951$, while the Monte Carlo test frequency is $0.5788$. The corresponding probabilities for scenario, unbiased KDE, and biased KDE decisions are approximately $0.0043$, $0.0450$, and $0.0107$. The training values in the table are computed on the optimization sample and the test values on an independent sample; they must not be conflated. The sample frequencies are therefore plausible finite-sample measurements, and the objective ranking is not an artifact of an unknown test distribution. The scenario solution is most conservative; the biased KDE is closer to it than the unbiased KDE in violation while retaining slightly lower objective than scenario. This is a single synthetic balance experiment and says nothing about network congestion, unit commitment, temporal correlation, or capacity uncertainty.

\paragraph*{Joint-event statistics.}
The joint experiment compares statistics rather than optimized risk allocations. Equal-Boole allocation and the exact maximum statistic use the same Boolean event in the current runner, so their held-out rates coincide at $0.5530$. The log-sum-exp statistic with $\tau=0.1$ gives $0.5745$. These values are computed on the held-out half of a 4000-sample pool; a separate ledger field averages over the full pool and must not be confused with the test rate. Component rates are approximately $0.3328$ and $0.3255$. The joint experiment therefore demonstrates how a smooth upper-envelope statistic changes the measured event, but it does not demonstrate a Boole allocation theorem or a joint chance-constrained optimizer. An actual allocation study would impose separate component constraints and report the union event on an independent test set.

\paragraph*{Dispatch sample-size sequence.}
The dispatch sequence provides a second check because its mixture CDF is known exactly. For the paper-aligned unbiased KDE, the objective decreases from $934.1317$ at $N=250$ to $933.6669$ and $933.4633$ at $N=1000$ and $N=5000$; test violations are $0.0347$, $0.0646$, and $0.0798$. For the paper-aligned biased KDE, the objective is $937.3410$, $936.1048$, and $935.2208$, with test violations $0$, $0.0009$, and $0.0069$. Figure~\ref{fig:paper-aligned-dispatch} plots both sequences. The trend is more stable than in the static problem because the balance residual is affine and the dispatch objective is convex and increasing on the nonnegative box. This is the local problem closest to Schuster's structural assumptions, although the adapter still has only one stochastic balance residual and no repeated-seed convergence study. The higher objective and lower violation of the paper-aligned biased KDE are the expected price of the pointwise upper envelope, not evidence that the Gaussian estimator is inconsistent.

The dispatch results also answer a practical question: does a paper-aligned bandwidth rule merely alter the estimator, or does it move the decision between the regimes of Table~\ref{tab:dispatch-results}? The held-out violations of the paper-aligned unbiased KDE sequence, $0.0347$--$0.0798$, lie between the nominal and scenario test frequencies of $0.5788$ and $0.0060$, while those of the paper-aligned biased KDE sequence, $0$--$0.0069$, stay close to the scenario level. Exact mixture probabilities were evaluated only for the four decisions of Table~\ref{tab:dispatch-results}, so this comparison rests on held-out frequencies. The conclusion is therefore about a trade-off between smoothness and one-sided protection. It is not a claim that either method dominates under temporal renewable uncertainty, since the adapter contains no time correlation or network constraints.

They show instead that a locally assembled pipeline must expose transcription invariants and solver status before numerical comparisons are meaningful.

\subsection{Evidence Summary}
\label{sec:experiment-evidence}
The observations in this chapter are conditional on the declared synthetic laws,
seeded sample splits, and solver statuses. The claim contract and validation
layers that govern how these observations may be read are defined in
\cref{sec:claim-contract,sec:validation-layers}; Chapter~10 interprets them.
