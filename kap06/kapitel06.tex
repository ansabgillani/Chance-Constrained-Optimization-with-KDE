\section{Optimal Control Formulation}
\label{ch:optimalcontrol}

The static chance-constrained problem considered in the preceding chapters extends
to an optimal-control problem by taking the decision to be a state--control
trajectory. Let $y(t)\in\R^{n_y}$, $u(t)\in\R^{n_u}$, and let $\xi$ denote the
uncertain parameter. A representative formulation is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \min_{y,u}\quad &J(y,u)=\int_0^{t_f} L(y(t),u(t))\,dt+\Phi(y(t_f)),\\
    \text{s.t.}\quad &\dot y(t)=F(y(t),u(t),\xi),\\
    &y(0)\in\mathcal Y_0,\quad u(t)\in\mathcal U,\\
    &\Prob\{g_k(y,u,\xi)\leq 0\}\geq 1-\varepsilon_k,
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:ocp-general}
\end{equation}
where path and terminal events may have different risk levels. The KDE reformulation
is applied to the scalar residuals at the points where the transcription evaluates
the constraints. For training samples $\xi_i$, the residuals are
$r_i(x)=g(x,\xi_i)$, and the deterministic approximation is the integrated-kernel
average introduced in Chapters~3--5. The resulting finite-dimensional problem is
an ordinary nonlinear program (NLP).

\subsection{Direct transcription and collocation}

Choose a mesh $0=t_0<\dots<t_N=t_f$ and represent the state and control by nodal
variables. With a step length $h_j=t_{j+1}-t_j$, a forward-Euler transcription has
the defect constraints
\begin{equation}
  y_{j+1}-y_j-h_jF(y_j,u_j,\xi)=0,\qquad j=0,\dots,N-1.
  \label{eq:euler-defect}
\end{equation}
Higher-order Radau or Hermite--Simpson defects can replace \cref{eq:euler-defect}
without changing the chance-constraint layer. The implementation keeps the
transcription separate from kernels and baselines: the solver receives residual
callables, while `kde`, `constraints`, `problems`, `solvers`, and `evaluation`
provide their numerical meanings. This separation makes a numerical OCP
limitation distinguishable from a failure of the static evidence.

\subsection{Soft lunar landing benchmark}
\label{sec:lunar-ocp}

The benchmark uses altitude $y_1$, vertical velocity $y_2$, and nonnegative thrust
$u$. In the nondimensional model,
\begin{equation}
  \dot y_1=y_2,\qquad \dot y_2=-1.622+u,
  \qquad u(t)\geq 0.
  \label{eq:lunar-dynamics}
\end{equation}
The objective is fuel, $J=\int_0^{t_f}u(t)\,dt$, with prescribed initial state
and terminal velocity $y_2(t_f)=0$. Terminal altitude is governed by the chance
event, not fixed by a second equality. The terminal position event
uses a Gaussian disturbance $\xi_1\sim\mathcal N(0,0.1^2)$ and tolerance
$\delta=0.25$:
\begin{equation}
  \Prob\left(\left|y_1(t_f)-\xi_1\right|-\delta>0\right)\leq\varepsilon_a,
  \qquad \varepsilon_a=0.1.
  \label{eq:lunar-terminal-risk}
\end{equation}
The path thrust event is
\begin{equation}
  \Prob\left(u(t_j)+\xi_2-u_{\max}>0\right)\leq\varepsilon_b,
  \qquad \varepsilon_b=0.01,
  \label{eq:lunar-path-risk}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_2$ is a documented bimodal Gaussian mixture and $u_{\max}=3$. In the
local experiment, $\xi_2$ has weights $(0.6,0.4)$, means $(-0.25,0.35)$, and
standard deviations $(0.10,0.15)$ in normalized power units. The local proxy
uses $t_f=20$ and initial state $(100,0)$. These equations follow the benchmark structure of
\citet{keil2020,keil2021}; the mesh, mixture weights, and numerical bounds used in
the local experiment are recorded with the result artifact rather than attributed
to the published study.

At each relevant collocation node, the implementation evaluates the terminal or
path residual on the training samples and applies an unbiased or biased integrated
KDE constraint. The continuation sequence is nominal, worst-case, scenario,
unbiased KDE, and the local shifted Epanechnikov surrogate. Each stage receives
the last successful solution as a warm start and records objective, iterations,
runtime, and solver status; failures and exceptions remain records. The local
implementation uses a SciPy direct-collocation fallback, so all stages are run
locally. A CasADi/IPOPT cross-check is not implemented in this adapter and no
result from that stack is reported.

\subsection{Implementation strategy}

The static nonlinear and energy-dispatch benchmarks are solved first because their
SciPy-based NLPs isolate statistical and bandwidth effects. The lunar transcription
then reuses the same residual, KDE, and validation interfaces. The local default
mesh has 11 state nodes and uses forward Euler defects. This order permits
the numerical limitations of collocation to be reported separately from the
statistical conclusions supported by the lower-dimensional experiments.
